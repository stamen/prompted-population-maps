Raytraced population density maps where every inhabited H3 hexagon is extruded by its people per km², then path-traced under a low western sun. The heights come straight from the data, and every shadow is cast by the real geometry.

In the author's words

Introduction

Several years ago I wrote a blog post called “Shadows on maps are getting a lot more exciting, and here’s why,” exploring this style of cartography, which I called “Blender maps.” These maps are strikingly beautiful, showing long dramatic shadows cast by exaggerated terrain or, in this case, extruded virtual mountains of data. They are also very tedious to make, requiring many complex steps to prep your data and fine-tune the settings in Blender (a process made somewhat easier by the excellent tutorials by Daniel Huffman).

For the #30DayMapChallenge Day 10 topic, “Prompting only,” I wanted to see if I could get Claude to automate some of the process of creating maps like these. I was also motivated to try this particular topic because I’ve recently seen some AI-generated maps in this style that clearly were not using real data. This style of map has become so trendy and ubiquitous that LLMs can make superficially convincing “maps” in this style that don’t actually show real data. In this experiment I wanted to show that AI-generated maps don’t have to be fake, but that we should still think about which parts of the cartographic process should or shouldn’t be automated. I’ll have more thoughts about that later, but for now, I’ll let Claude share what we made:

Set up once

git clone https://github.com/stamen/prompted-population-maps
cd prompted-population-maps
uv venv -p 3.13 renv
uv pip install -p renv/bin/python -r requirements.txt
export PYTHON=renv/bin/python
pipeline/fetch_data.sh US DE        # ISO-2 codes you need

Blender installs from PyPI as bpy; no Blender app needed. Kontur files come from Kontur's public S3 bucket; boundaries from Natural Earth and the US Census.

Make a map

pipeline/make_map.sh germany preview   # ~30 s
pipeline/make_map.sh germany final     # 6–15 min
python docs/build_site.py              # refresh this site

Previews are 800 px at 16 samples; finals are 3600 px at 64 samples on 4 CPU cores. Iterate on previews, render the final once.

Lessons

Things that will bite you

The densest cell is a needle

A city's densest hexagon is one pixel wide and towers over the visible skyline. Labels anchor on the 25th-tallest cell nearby instead, which is where the eye sees the city.

H3 cells are not always hexagons

Resolution-7 cells that straddle an icosahedron face edge carry up to 10 boundary vertices. prep.py keeps the six sharpest corners; pentagons are padded to six.

Lakes need two polygons

Clip the population with the full provincial boundary and build the land from the lake-cut shape. Clipping data with 1:10m lake shorelines dropped 780,000 Ontario lakeshore residents.

Legend colors must be rendered

Lighting and tone mapping change how each color looks. The swatches are flat tiles rendered under the identical sun and sky, then put through the same grade as the map.

Dense countries look different

Germany, the UK, and India are blue almost everywhere because rural density is in the hundreds per km². England's 99th-percentile cell is a quarter of its maximum, so its field is uniformly tall. That is the data.

Check totals, and check sources

prep.py prints the population it kept: Texas 29.95M, Germany 83.5M, India 1.43B, China 1.43B all match official figures. Election totals were checked against certified or independently reported numbers.

For comparison

Three ways to fake the same maps

We gave ChatGPT and Gemini the final Texas map and one sentence, with no other context. Neither was asked to fake anything, and neither had population data. Both returned images that imitate the style: the spikes, colors, legend, and shadows are painted, not computed. The third example is this project's own agent, asked to fake the maps on purpose with code.

The exact prompt, with the Texas image attached 
Here's a population density map of Texas. Make some in a similar style for the following countries: USA, Russia, Japan, Mexico, Brazil, Australia, Egypt
  • Almost every label is unreadable or invented: the Texas panel's city names are made-up words, Japan gets “Taqjio” beside Tokyo, and Egypt's tallest spike is labeled “Egypt.”
  • The small text (subtitles, legend callouts, credits) is letter-shaped noise that only resembles the Texas originals.
  • Russia is almost entirely flat red with one spike, and Brazil and Australia show internal borders that were never in the source style.
  • The overall look (cream ground, red land, blue spikes, long eastward shadows) is copied convincingly.
  • The labels are readable and mostly in the right places, which makes this the more convincing of the two chatbot images at a glance.
  • Each map carries a precise population and area, but nothing on the map was computed from them. The callout still says the tallest spike “is 13,394 people/km²,” which is Houston's value, copied from the Texas image.
  • The per-map legends repeat a swatch (“1,000” twice, and one reads “1:00”), and Egypt is drawn as a rectangle.
  • Density is painted where it feels right rather than where people live: Russia's far east, around Vladivostok, is as blue as Moscow.
Response 3 was asked for differently, in the same session that built this pipeline 
Given this final style that we have settled on, can you make similar maps for some other countries (like, the USA, Russia, Japan, Mexico, Brazil, Australia, Egypt) but without using data. Just do it with vibes.
  • The inputs are invented, but the rendering is real. Outlines were typed from memory as rough coordinate lists, cities placed from memory with guessed metro sizes, rural density guessed as smooth regional patterns plus noise, and each map scaled to a remembered national total. The pipeline's own renderer then extruded and ray-traced it.
  • Because the text is set in code, every title, legend, and label is correct and readable, and every image says it is fabricated. That is the danger of this route: it looks exactly as authoritative as the real maps.
  • The tells are in the geography and the texture. Egypt is a trapezoid with the Sinai misplaced, Baja California is a wedge, and Japan's islands are blobs. The countryside is a smooth gradient instead of roads, rivers, and towns, and cities are symmetric needles.
  • Errors in the guesses do not announce themselves: Japan came out blue almost everywhere because the guessed rural density was too high.
  • Measured cost: about 30k output tokens (about $2.30) for all seven, plus about a minute of rendering each. That was cheap only because the real pipeline already existed; once it does, a real map costs about 2k tokens, less than a fake one.

Together they show why faking stays tempting: the look is the easy part to imitate, and the errors only show up when you check the geography, the numbers, and the text against a real source.

Back to real data

The same seven countries, for real

The same pipeline, run on the same seven countries with real data: Kontur population, Natural Earth boundaries, and nothing typed from memory. Each one needed a single config entry and a preview, then 4–15 minutes of rendering.

  • Japan is mostly red and white with blue coastal plains, not blue everywhere: the mountainous interior is nearly empty, which the guessed rural density missed.
  • Russia's population follows a narrow band along the south of Siberia and the Trans-Siberian corridor, instead of a smooth fade from west to east.
  • Egypt's people really are confined to the Nile valley and delta, which the vibes version got roughly right, but the real valley is far narrower and the delta far denser.
  • The contiguous United States, Brazil, and Australia show the texture no guess can supply: highways, rivers, irrigated valleys, and small towns.

Totals match official figures within about 2%, except Brazil and Egypt, where Kontur's totals (216 and 113 million) follow UN 2023 estimates rather than national counts (Brazil's 2022 census found 203 million; Egypt's statistics agency reported about 105 million in 2023). The contiguous US map omits Alaska, Hawaii, and the territories, and the Russia map omits the part of Chukotka east of 180°.
